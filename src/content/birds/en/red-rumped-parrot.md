## Meet the bird

Typical males are green with yellowish lower areas and a red rump. Females are usually duller green; mutations can alter the picture. Natural range: Open woodland and grassland in southeastern Australia.

## Recognising the species

The red rump is not visible in every pose. Consider age, plumage and documented identity rather than judging from one angle.

## Behaviour and companionship

It forages on the ground and flies actively. An apparently quiet pair can still defend territory, so small-bird mixing needs caution.

## Housing, movement and cleanup

Provide long flight space, clean flooring and shelter. Offer floor-foraging food in clean trays rather than dirty substrate.

## Planning its diet

Use a balanced small grass-parrot diet. A long list of seed names does not make a mix nutritionally complete.

## Pairing and breeding welfare

Conflict can intensify when guarding nests or chicks. Plan observation, separate housing where needed and recovery between breeding attempts.

## Before bringing one home

Choose an independently feeding bird with documented identity and responsible provenance. Keep new birds separate and discuss an initial examination with a veterinarian. Persistent fluffed posture, breathing difficulty or stopping eating need prompt assessment. Do not take an unweaned chick and start inexperienced hand-feeding.
