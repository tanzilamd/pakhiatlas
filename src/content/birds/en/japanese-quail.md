## Identity and varieties

A ground-dwelling domestic bird, not a miniature parrot. Japanese quail and Chinese painted/button quail are different birds; clarify the scientific identity rather than relying on market names.

Natural ancestry: Domesticated lineage of an East Asian quail.

## Living space and companionship

Provide dry litter, sheltered floor space, traction, cover and protection from predators. Quail may jump upward when startled; enclosure design must reduce head injury. Do not use a parrot cage with high perches as a complete quail setup.

## Feeding and daily care

Choose a nutritionally appropriate quail ration for the life stage and reproductive state. A finch seed mix is not a substitute. Avoid guessed homemade protein or calcium percentages. Use accessible feed and water containers that minimise contamination, and inspect bedding around them for dampness and spoilage.

## Pairing, nesting and parent welfare

[Before the nest: pairing and breeding readiness](../../guides/breeding-readiness/)

Early egg production is not proof of good welfare or a reason to keep increasing light. Nutrition and reproductive management need a species-specific plan. Do not assume a laying female needs a male or that domestic birds will reliably incubate eggs.

## Droppings, odour and cleaning

Floor litter needs frequent inspection because droppings, spilled water and feed collect in the same area. Remove wet patches and soiled material promptly; ventilation alone does not clean bedding. Plan litter storage and disposal before acquisition. New abnormal odour or persistent droppings changes need investigation.
