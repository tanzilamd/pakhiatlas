## Meet the bird

An Australian cockatoo with a pink face and breast, grey wings and a pale crest. It is not a pink colour mutation of the cockatiel. Natural range: Open woodlands, grasslands and agricultural habitats of Australia.

## Recognising the species

Both flight and ground foraging are normal activities. Attractive colour comes with a strong beak, loud calls and powder down.

## Behaviour and companionship

A social bird may not thrive on evening affection after being left unstimulated all day. Plan companionship and activity around the household’s real schedule.

## Housing, movement and cleanup

Provide robust spacious housing, flight opportunities and clean floor-foraging trays. Do not scatter food into dirty substrate.

## Planning its diet

Avoid making high-fat seeds the main food. Monitor variety, exercise, weight and actual consumption so changes can be recognised.

## Pairing and breeding welfare

A pair and a box are not sufficient for responsible breeding. Plan health, compatibility, enclosure safety and future chick care without pushing repeated breeding.

## Before bringing one home

Choose an independently feeding bird with documented identity and responsible provenance. Keep new birds separate and discuss an initial examination with a veterinarian. Persistent fluffed posture, breathing difficulty or stopping eating need prompt assessment. Do not take an unweaned chick and start inexperienced hand-feeding.
