## Meet the bird

A substantial green Amazon parrot, often with yellow facial areas and blue on the forehead. The amount of face colour varies. Natural range: Woodlands, savannas and wooded habitats in South America.

## Recognising the species

Do not identify every green parrot as this species. Match body form, facial markings and documented identity. A promise of speech is not a sound acquisition criterion.

## Behaviour and companionship

Expect loud calls, a strong beak and individual preferences. Read posture before touching and stop when uncomfortable. Behaviour may change with sexual maturity.

## Housing, movement and cleanup

Provide robust housing, secure latches, chewable enrichment and supervised exercise space. Closely supervise any contact with children or other animals.

## Planning its diet

Plan a balanced diet rather than relying on fatty seeds and nuts. Monitor selective eating and weight trends; do not force a sudden food switch.

## Pairing and breeding welfare

Do not use nests or dark cavities to manage behaviour. Breeding requires experienced support, pair safety and plans for future offspring.

## Before bringing one home

Choose an independently feeding bird with documented identity and responsible provenance. Keep new birds separate and discuss an initial examination with a veterinarian. Persistent fluffed posture, breathing difficulty or stopping eating need prompt assessment. Do not take an unweaned chick and start inexperienced hand-feeding.
