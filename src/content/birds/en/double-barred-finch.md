## Meet the bird

A small finch with a pale face framed by dark markings, two dark chest bands and fine pale wing spots. Natural range: Grassy woodland-edge habitats in northern and eastern Australia.

## Recognising the species

The “owl finch” name does not make it an owl, or a zebra-finch mutation. It has its own species identity.

## Behaviour and companionship

It suits observation of flight and social contact. Do not impose handling expectations; introduce compatible companions carefully.

## Housing, movement and cleanup

Even a tiny bird needs horizontal flight room. Start conservatively rather than mixing with larger or food-dominant birds.

## Planning its diet

Offer a balanced small-finch diet. Check for husk-filled bowls, dirty water and damp fallen food every day.

## Pairing and breeding welfare

Nesting materials must not create entanglement hazards. Prioritise health, rest and future chick care rather than treating eggs as the purpose of keeping the bird.

## Before bringing one home

Choose an independently feeding bird with documented identity and responsible provenance. Keep new birds separate and discuss an initial examination with a veterinarian. Persistent fluffed posture, breathing difficulty or stopping eating need prompt assessment. Do not take an unweaned chick and start inexperienced hand-feeding.
