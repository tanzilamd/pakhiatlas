## Meet the bird

Typical adult males show a blue face, scarlet chest and yellow belly. Females and young birds do not share exactly the same appearance. Natural range: Dry inland scrub and woodland-edge habitats of Australia.

## Recognising the species

The name does not make it a scarlet macaw. It is a small grass parrot with different housing and feeding needs.

## Behaviour and companionship

Provide calm conditions and freedom to move at its own pace. Build familiarity through predictable routines and food rewards rather than chasing.

## Housing, movement and cleanup

Provide flight space, shelter and clean dry feeding areas. Avoid persistently damp floors and crowded cages.

## Planning its diet

Use a balanced small-parakeet feeding plan. A colourful seed mix is not proof of completeness; loss of normal appetite deserves attention.

## Pairing and breeding welfare

Plan pair safety, health and recovery time. Colour demand is not a reason for repeated breeding or premature chick separation.

## Before bringing one home

Choose an independently feeding bird with documented identity and responsible provenance. Keep new birds separate and discuss an initial examination with a veterinarian. Persistent fluffed posture, breathing difficulty or stopping eating need prompt assessment. Do not take an unweaned chick and start inexperienced hand-feeding.
