## Meet the bird

A small Australian grass parrot with a blue-toned face, green back and yellowish underparts. It is not the turquoise colour mutation of a green-cheeked conure. Natural range: Open woodland and grassy habitats in eastern Australia.

## Recognising the species

Typical sexes can differ in colour, but age and colour mutations complicate identification. Do not make a final decision from facial blue alone.

## Behaviour and companionship

Calm observation and gradual introduction suit this bird better than forced handling. Do not house it with larger or assertive parrots simply because it is small.

## Housing, movement and cleanup

Provide horizontal flight space, shelter and multiple perches. Check that the bird can reach food and water without being bullied.

## Planning its diet

Use a balanced plan appropriate for a small grass parrot. Verify actual consumption when introducing food; a filled bowl does not prove acceptance.

## Pairing and breeding welfare

Breeding requires calm conditions and a compatible pair. In shared aviaries, watch chasing and exclusion from food rather than assuming all pairs coexist safely.

## Before bringing one home

Choose an independently feeding bird with documented identity and responsible provenance. Keep new birds separate and discuss an initial examination with a veterinarian. Persistent fluffed posture, breathing difficulty or stopping eating need prompt assessment. Do not take an unweaned chick and start inexperienced hand-feeding.
