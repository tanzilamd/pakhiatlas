## Meet the bird

A small ground bird. Typical males have a blue-grey breast and black-and-white throat markings; females are browner. Pale captive forms do not show every typical marking. Natural range: Grassy habitats across southern and southeastern Asia and into Australasia.

## Recognising the species

Older sources use Coturnix chinensis or Excalfactoria chinensis. This is a separate species, not a small breed of Japanese quail.

## Behaviour and companionship

Provide cover that lets a ground bird feel secure. Design the roof with sudden upward panic flight in mind.

## Housing, movement and cleanup

Usable floor area, dry substrate, cover and accessible water matter. Do not use a quail as a cleanup device for another aviary.

## Planning its diet

Use a complete quail-appropriate feeding plan. Fallen finch seeds alone should not be assumed to meet nutritional needs.

## Pairing and breeding welfare

Assess compatibility by observation. Egg laying is not a reason to encourage continuous breeding; weakness or difficulty laying requires prompt veterinary help.

## Before bringing one home

Choose an independently feeding bird with documented identity and responsible provenance. Keep new birds separate and discuss an initial examination with a veterinarian. Persistent fluffed posture, breathing difficulty or stopping eating need prompt assessment. Do not take an unweaned chick and start inexperienced hand-feeding.
