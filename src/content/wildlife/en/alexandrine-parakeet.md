## Name and identification

A large long-tailed parakeet, distinct from the rose-ringed parakeet. The shoulder patch is an identification clue; generic market names alone can confuse the two.

## Behaviour and needs

Its long tail, strong beak and activity make housing and safe exercise important considerations. Species identity, origin and individual history matter more than a seller’s talking or tameness claim.

## Droppings and hygiene

The liquid component can vary with soft or water-rich foods; colour and wetness alone cannot identify a disease. Observe the individual’s normal feces, urates and urine on clean plain lining. Moist food and accumulated waste add to the cleaning burden. If a bird appears unwell, do not wait for an online colour or odour match before seeking help.

## Colour, age and market names

Different species may be sold under similar names, while age, sex and colour mutations may make one species look different. Colour, neck markings or talking claims alone do not establish age, sex or captive origin. When someone says “yellow parrot”, first establish which species and colour form they mean.

## Observation and assistance

This is a native/wild-bird educational entry, not a recommendation to acquire one. Market presence does not establish lawful captive provenance. Observe from a distance and leave nests and young undisturbed. An injured bird needs qualified wildlife or veterinary assistance; do not force food or water.
