## Let the bird choose

Birds differ in how they bathe. Offer a suitable shallow bathing opportunity or gentle mist only if the bird accepts it. Keep escape from the water easy and observe throughout. Do not submerge, restrain or chase a frightened bird into bathing. Keep food and drinking water free from bath waste.

## Dry safely and avoid products

Let the bird dry in a safe comfortable area without exposure to extreme cold or heat. Do not use perfumed sprays, human shampoo, oils or improvised feather conditioners. Heat appliances and household fumes create avoidable hazards. A bird that is unwell needs an individual veterinary plan rather than extra bathing.

## Molt is a process, not a diagnosis

Normal feather replacement and new feather growth can change appearance and daily behaviour. Continue appropriate balanced feeding, rest and enrichment; do not assume every molt needs supplements. Do not pull developing feathers or force touch on sensitive areas. Keep a record of changes rather than diagnosing from a single fallen feather.

## When feather changes need help

Bald areas, damaged skin, bleeding or persistent feather destruction deserve assessment. Medical and environmental factors can overlap. Do not punish the bird or assume a new toy cures every problem. Active bleeding, marked weakness or breathing difficulty needs urgent attention.
