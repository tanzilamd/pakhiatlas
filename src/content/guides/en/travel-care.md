## Arrange the caregiver first

Leaving a full food bowl is not a care plan. Decide who will change water, wash bowls, observe real intake, clean and watch behaviour. Let the caregiver meet the bird beforehand.

Do not start new diets, toys, cage sharing or training experiments during an absence. A familiar safe routine is easier to maintain.

## What to put on one page

- The bird's name, species, identification and usual behaviour.
- Familiar food, preparation and storage. Use the individual's established plan, not another keeper's spoon allowance.
- Water, bowl, cage and rest routines.
- Changes in intake or behaviour that require contacting you and the veterinarian.
- Bird-experienced veterinary contacts, address and backup emergency arrangements.
- Location of the secure carrier, care budget and health records.

Demonstrate the daily tasks and let the caregiver practise rather than only sending a list. Do not assign hand restraint to someone uncomfortable handling the bird.

## Staying home or moving elsewhere

At another home, reassess fumes, sprays, fans, windows, children and other animals. Avoid mixing with resident birds or sharing bowls; plan health screening and separate accommodation.

Write down the safe alternative for outages, heat or rain. Do not leave the cage in direct sun or an overheated closed room and rely on somebody rescuing the situation later.

## Prepare the carrier

Choose a sturdy, securely closing, ventilated carrier. Check sharp edges, escape gaps and spilling bowls. Introduce voluntary approach and entry calmly before an illness makes transport urgent.

Secure the carrier in the vehicle, protect it from direct sun and strong cold airflow, and never leave a bird alone in a parked car. Do not open it for affection while travelling. Include the weight and beak strength of a large bird in the plan.

## Going to the veterinarian

Call ahead to confirm bird care, destination and transport advice. Bring records of the current diet, symptom onset, previous illness and medication. Ask whether useful photographs or a dropping sample are needed.

Avoid repeatedly catching and examining an ill bird, forcing food or water and guessing medication. Do not delay travel for an online answer when breathing difficulty, major bleeding or severe weakness is present.

## After returning

Check intake, movement and droppings against the usual pattern and read the caregiver's notes. Discuss a health plan after contact with other birds. Read [separation and introductions](../quarantine/) and [Bangladesh weather care](../bangladesh-weather/) as well.
