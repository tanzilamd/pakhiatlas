## Before you visit

Prepare secure housing, food, a carrier, a separate arrival area and a bird-experienced clinic contact. Decide how daily time, noise, travel and ongoing costs will be managed. Do not let a rare colour or a low price replace this plan.

## Questions to ask

Ask for identity, hatch date or age uncertainty, current food and actual independent eating, health history, prior treatment, contact with other birds and available records. Ask to see normal activity without provoking the bird. A seller’s “DNA sexed” claim should have traceable records if relevant. Never select an unweaned chick as a beginner bonding project.

## What observation cannot prove

Bright eyes and active behavior do not rule out disease. Conversely, a single photograph cannot diagnose disease or determine exact age. Crowding, dirty water, fear and inability to access food are welfare concerns worth asking about. Avoid impulse acquisition; plan professional assessment and quarantine. Do not assume an offered native wild bird is a lawful captive-bred pet.

## Responsible rehoming

Share honest health, diet and behavior information, records, current routines and known uncertainties. Confirm the new household can provide safe care and veterinary access. Use secure, ventilated transport and avoid temperature extremes. This site provides education, not listings or transactions.
