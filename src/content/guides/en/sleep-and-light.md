## Make rest predictable

Plan a regular quiet rest period away from television, repeated handling and late-night household activity. Keep the bird’s needs and household routine in mind rather than changing lighting at random. Daytime activity and sleep both matter; a covered cage should not become all-day isolation.

## Use covers thoughtfully

If a cover is used, ensure ventilation remains adequate, the fabric cannot be pulled into the enclosure and the bird is comfortable. Do not use covering as punishment for calling. If a bird startles at night, inspect sound, shadows, other animals and collision hazards before repeatedly grabbing or rearranging it.

## Light does not replace a care plan

Do not extend light exposure to obtain more eggs or song. Artificial lighting and any specialist lamp need a species-appropriate plan and the product’s safe-use guidance, not guessed distance or duration. Direct sun through a window can also overheat an enclosure. Keep shaded choices and safe ventilation available.

## Notice a new pattern

A suddenly sleepy, withdrawn or unusually restless bird may need assessment of health as well as environment. Record appetite, droppings and activity alongside sleep changes. A breathing problem or collapse is urgent, not something to solve by changing a cage cover.
