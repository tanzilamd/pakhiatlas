## Combine identification clues

One name or photograph cannot establish every aspect of identity. Compare the face, beak, body shape, tail, plumage, calls and observation location together. Young and adult birds or males and females of one species can look different. A claim of speech does not establish age or origin.

“Yellow parrot” is an ambiguous market name; establish the actual species and colour form first. Myna identity is not determined by talking ability either. Use the individual species profiles and photographs for comparison.

## Observation and acquisition are different decisions

PakhiAtlas native-bird profiles help readers recognise birds in nature. Market presence does not establish lawful captive-bred origin. Provenance records and applicable requirements are separate matters; colour and tameness are not proof of origin.

Observe from a distance. Do not obstruct nests, young or feeding routes for a photograph. Repeatedly startling or chasing a bird closer is not good observation.

## Injured birds or birds in danger

Obtain qualified wildlife or bird-experienced veterinary help. Avoid repeated catching, forced food or water and improvised medication. Keep rescued wildlife separate from household birds.

Explain where the bird was found, what happened and what signs you observed. A useful photograph can assist, but should not delay obtaining help.
