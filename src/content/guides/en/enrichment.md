## Start with natural movement

Finches need useful flight space; quail need suitable ground activity and cover; parrots need movement plus appropriate manipulation and chewing. Do not choose activities solely because another species enjoys them. Keep exits, fans, other pets and hazards controlled during supervised exercise.

## Make food a small task

Begin with an easy, visible food opportunity the bird already understands. Gradually add a safe simple challenge while keeping ordinary food available. Watch actual intake. A puzzle that prevents eating is not enrichment. Avoid loose fibres, sharp parts, unsafe metals and openings that can trap a head or foot.

## Introduce and rotate thoughtfully

Let a cautious bird inspect a new item from a comfortable distance. Reward voluntary investigation. Rotate a few safe activities instead of suddenly changing the whole enclosure. Keep useful movement space clear and inspect worn toys every day.

## Measure benefit by behavior

Look for relaxed exploration, successful feeding, rest and normal interaction rather than constant activity. Stop an activity that causes persistent fear or guarding. Sudden withdrawal, destructive feather behavior or major changes warrant assessment of health as well as environment; more toys alone are not a diagnosis or cure.
