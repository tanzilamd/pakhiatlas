## Decide what role seed will play

One kilogram recipe cannot be assumed appropriate for budgies, cockatiels, lovebirds and conures. Many seed types do not guarantee vitamin and mineral balance. First decide the species-appropriate staple, the role of seed and how dietary changes will be monitored.

Preparing a mix is different from formulating a nutritionally complete ration. The calculator above scales one published budgie seed-portion composition into grams. Batch size is not a daily feeding allowance.

## Step 1: identify actual ingredients

Local labels such as china, kaun and different millets may overlap. A “four-millet mix” does not automatically add four distinct nutritional benefits. Check the identity, food quality and suitability of canary seed, paddy, barley or wheat before including them.

Do not add ambiguously named ingredients without identifying them. Agricultural seeds treated for planting are not bird food. Ask about storage history rather than judging only the appearance.

## Step 2: make a small batch

Several kilograms may take two birds a long time to consume. In Dhaka’s heat and humidity, choose a batch size that turns over rather than keeping old food indefinitely. Use clean dry containers, a scale and a dated ingredient record.

Inspect each ingredient for dust, insects, webbing, damp odour and mold. Float or germination tests do not prove freedom from toxins or contamination.

## Step 3: weigh and record

Weigh the ingredients selected within the bird’s feeding plan and combine them dry in a clean vessel. Record each amount and the total so the next batch is recognisable. Mixing several unlabeled commercial blends makes consumption harder to interpret.

Do not fill most of the mix with energy-dense sunflower, safflower or similar seeds. Keep training rewards separately and notice if handfuls of treats are quietly expanding daily intake. Do not improvise medication, oil or vitamin additions.

## Step 4: protect dry storage

Use clean dry covered containers away from heat, steam and wet scoops. Empty and clean before topping up, and label which batch should be used first.

Do not salvage moldy food by washing, sun-drying or mixing it with fresh seed. Discard the affected batch. Soaked and sprouted foods need different hygiene and must never be returned to dry storage.

## Step 5: monitor what is eaten

A selective eater does not consume ingredients in the proportions present in the bowl. Remove husks, check edible food, provide separate feeding positions and monitor weight and behaviour. Never remove familiar food to starve a bird into accepting something new.

Read the [cockatiel feeding guide](../cockatiel-feeding/) for vegetables, fruit, legumes and grit. Do not copy this parrot-seed approach for nectar-feeding lorikeets or quail.
