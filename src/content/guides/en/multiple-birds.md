## Prepare separate housing first

Before adding a bird, have secure separate housing, utensils and a health-screening plan. Discuss quarantine arrangements with a veterinarian; a fixed number of days alone does not rule out all disease. Do not use the resident bird as a test of whether the newcomer is healthy.

## Let introductions be gradual

After health concerns are addressed, allow controlled observation without forcing contact. Both birds must be able to withdraw. Keep initial interactions supervised and stop if one repeatedly chases, corners or injures the other. Larger beaks can seriously injure small birds, and small birds can also be territorial.

## Check access, not just fighting

A bird may lose access to food or rest without an obvious fight. Watch each individual eating and settling. Arrange enough suitable feeding and resting opportunities and avoid crowding. A nest site or valuable item can change interactions; do not add one to repair an incompatible relationship.

## Separate housing can be the answer

Do not force a pairing because the birds were bought together or labelled male and female. Keep health and ancestry records, observe changes and plan long-term separation if necessary. A compatible companion does not remove the need for daily care, enrichment and veterinary access.
