## One name is not one price

A species label alone is not a comparable quote. Ordinary and exhibition budgies, grey and selected-colour cockatiels, and peach-faced versus Fischer’s lovebirds differ in identity. Green-cheeked, sun and Jenday conures are distinct species; pineapple and turquoise can describe green-cheeked colour forms.

Ask first whether a quote covers one bird or a pair. Establish independent feeding, evidence for age and identity, and known health or behaviour issues. “Hand-tame” and “breeding pair” are claims to examine, not proof.

## Compare matching information in Dhaka

A market, aviary or online listing may use a general photograph rather than the actual bird. Compare current images, dates, age evidence, sexing method and price unit. Do not let an unusually low quote replace checks on health and responsible provenance.

Record the date and area. An old advertised asking price is not today’s completed sale price. This site does not provide seller contacts or transaction services.

## Costs beyond the bird

- Suitable housing or flight space, perches, food and water bowls.
- Safe enrichment, a transport carrier and cleaning supplies.
- An initial veterinary assessment and any indicated tests.
- Appropriate food, vegetables, replacement toys and continuing hygiene.
- A separate reserve for illness or urgent transport.

A small purchase price does not remove housing and veterinary needs. Feather powder from a cockatiel, loud conure calls or wet lorikeet-food cleanup also consume household time.

## Before paying extra for a label

Colour does not guarantee temperament, speech, health or breeding success. Treat “rare”, “proven pair” and guaranteed speech as claims requiring evidence. Buying an unweaned chick cheaply and taking on amateur hand-feeding risk is not a saving.

Native parakeets or mynas offered in markets should not be treated as ordinary pet purchases. Establish identity, provenance and applicable requirements; wild-bird trade prices are not provided here.
